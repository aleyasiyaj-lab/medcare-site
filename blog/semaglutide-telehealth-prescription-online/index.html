What Is Semaglutide and How Does It Work?

Semaglutide belongs to a class of medications called GLP-1 receptor agonists. It works by mimicking a natural hormone in your body that helps regulate appetite and blood sugar. By slowing gastric emptying and signaling fullness to the brain, semaglutide may help you eat less and feel satisfied longer. It’s important to note that this medication is typically prescribed for individuals with a body mass index of 30 or higher, or those with a BMI of 27 or higher who also have a weight-related condition, such as high blood pressure or high cholesterol.

Is Semaglutide Right for Everyone?

Not everyone is a candidate for semaglutide. It is generally not recommended for people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, or those with multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2. Pregnant or breastfeeding women should not take it. During your telehealth visit, your provider will review these and other contraindications thoroughly. The goal is to ensure you have all the information needed to make an informed decision—one that aligns with your health and lifestyle.

It’s also important to remember that semaglutide is not a quick fix. Sustainable weight loss typically requires changes in eating habits, physical activity, and sometimes behavior modification. Our team can connect you with resources and support to help you build long-term healthy habits.
